# Audience Committee

## What it does

Runs a finished artifact through a single adjudicated review from three constituencies at once — a sophisticated peer, a skeptical potential client, and a hiring manager — instead of three separate reviews. It falsifies weak claims, resolves disagreement between the three perspectives, and reports what actually survives scrutiny. It's an adjudicator, not an editor: it won't rewrite the artifact or assume revision is required.

## Why it exists

A single reviewer perspective misses things a different audience would catch — a peer cares about rigor, a potential client cares about whether they'd buy, a hiring manager cares about operating signal. Running all three as one deliberating committee, rather than three isolated passes, surfaces where they agree and where they don't, which is more useful than three disconnected opinions.

## When I use it

On a finished (or near-finished) piece of writing — a report, proposal, case study, or similar artifact — when I want a hard read on whether it holds up before it goes out the door, not a line edit.
